# Mark allocation rules

Category marks must be finite, non-negative and no greater than their effective maximum. Blank means unmarked; zero is a valid mark. AI `Marks: X/Y` suggestions must use the category's exact maximum as Y. Invalid suggestions are rejected without applying a mark.

Mark entry and the Students with Marks summary share `getAssessmentForMarking` in `src/utils/rubricMarks.js`. A valid maximum in the rubric’s last Marks column takes precedence over the configured category allocation, using the saved row-to-category mapping. Categories without a valid rubric maximum retain their configured allocation. Automatic resolution never rewrites stored allocations or student marks. The explicit “Fill category marks from table” action saves recognized rubric maxima into category settings, retaining unmatched allocations. It does not change awarded student marks or Total Marks. The Paragraphs header shows the sum of effective category allocations, so a genuine mismatch remains visible.

The assessment maximum is the positive configured total, or the sum of category maxima when no positive total is configured. A configured total that disagrees with the category allocations prevents a final percentage. Existing invalid marks are preserved for correction and flagged, rather than silently clamped. Duplicate category names also prevent completion.

A final percentage requires all categories with a positive allocation (or fixed marking mode) to be marked. Unknown legacy category marks are preserved in stored data but excluded from the current assessment total. Reports distinguish incomplete marking from a final result.

Weighted contribution is `(awarded / assessment maximum) × assessment weight`. Subject percentage divides summed contributions by summed positive assessment weights and multiplies by 100. Missing results for weighted assessments prevent a final subject grade. Numeric weights saved as strings (for example, `"60"`) are parsed before calculation. For assessments out of 100, marks of 80 at 40% and 90 at 60% contribute 32.0 and 54.0, yielding 86.0 overall (A).

Default letter grades are A+ from 90%, A from 85%, A- from 80%, B+ from 75%, B from 70%, B- from 65%, C+ from 60%, C from 55%, C- from 50%, D (Fail) from 40%, and F below 40%. The separate five-colour default rubric bands remain unchanged. Boundaries are lower-inclusive and upper-exclusive, except 100% is included. Legacy integer ranges ending at 39/49/64/79 extend to the next band's boundary, so fractional marks have no accidental gaps. Custom assessment bands are respected; unspecified custom letter grades display the color label rather than an invented letter. Subject results use the shared default scheme.

These rules establish consistent arithmetic. The educational suitability of category weights, institutional grade thresholds, and AI judgement still requires an assessor-approved rubric and comparison against human-marked examples.

RAG comment generation receives the category’s allocated mark (including zero), effective maximum, and saved rationale in both execution and prompt preview. It explains the existing decision using the full submission and rubric, preserves the score, and flags material evidence conflicts for assessor review. Missing marks or rationale are labelled as absent, not invented. This applies to both per-category and bulk RAG comments.

Recommended workflow: with no student selected, fill category maxima from the rubric in the Category mark setup panel. Load a student, run **1. Assign marks to all headings**, review the marks and rationale, then run **2. Improve all with RAG**. The setup panel is hidden during student marking.

“Clear all marks input boxes” asks for confirmation, then clears the current student's awarded category marks and associated rationales. It does not change category maxima, feedback paragraphs or comment drafts. Save Student Data to persist the cleared marks. When unfinished paragraphs exist, saving asks for confirmation and preserves quick-add text as drafts rather than adding it to feedback.

Student uploads accept PPTX presentations. Extraction follows presentation slide order and includes slide/table text, speaker notes, cached chart/diagram text and supported embedded slide images. The extracted content is included in AI marking alongside other student materials. This is content extraction, not a rendered PowerPoint viewer; animations, video and slide layout are not reproduced. Unsupported embedded image formats are identified in the extracted content.

When paragraph text is present, RAG generation treats its assessor observations and judgements as authoritative ground truth. It preserves those points ahead of conflicting saved rationale or model interpretations of the submission. Supporting material may expand consistent points but must not invent evidence. The numeric mark remains unchanged; a conflicting numeric judgement is flagged for assessor review. Improve English also preserves the supplied comments' factual meaning.
